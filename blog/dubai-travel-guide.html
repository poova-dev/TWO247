Best Time to Visit Dubai: Seasons, Weather, & Festivals Explained

By TWO247 Travel Desk • Published November 2026 • 7 Min Read

Dubai is a global marvel where futuristic skyscrapers tower over timeless golden dunes. Yet because the city lies in the Arabian Desert, timing your trip correctly makes the difference between breezy al fresco dining by Dubai Marina and retreating indoors to air-conditioned mega-malls.

1. The Peak Winter Season (November to March)

Without doubt, November through March represents the golden window for Dubai travel. Daytime temperatures hover between a delightful 24°C and 28°C, while evening breezes drop into the crisp high teens.

During these months, outdoor life in the emirate is at its zenith:

  • Desert Safaris: Comfortable conditions for sunset dune bashing and overnight Lahbab camps without scorching heat.
  • Marina & Beach Walks: Perfect weather for beachfront strolls along JBR and open-air rooftop lounges.
  • Outdoor Attractions: The Dubai Miracle Garden, Global Village, and Dubai Safari Park are in full operation.

Travel Tip: Because this is peak tourism season, flights and 5-star beach resorts book out rapidly. We advise securing your Luxury Dubai Escape Package at least 6 to 8 weeks in advance.

2. The Shoulder Months (April, May, September & October)

The shoulder seasons provide a superb balance between pleasant sea temperatures and attractive hotel pricing. Daytime highs reach 33°C to 37°C, but hotel swimming pools, luxury yacht charters, and shaded beach clubs remain thoroughly enjoyable.

If you want 5-star beachfront resorts on Palm Jumeirah at 30% to 40% lower rates than December, consider visiting during late October or April.

3. Dubai Shopping Festival (DSF)

Held annually across January and February, the Dubai Shopping Festival transforms the city into a celebration of fireworks, celebrity concerts, raffle draws, and citywide retail discounts up to 75%.

Essential Travel Checklist for Dubai

  1. Tourist Visa: Indian passport holders can easily obtain a 30-day or 60-day tourist visa through TWO247 within 48 to 72 hours.
  2. Dress Code: Resort wear and swimwear are completely normal at pools, private beaches, and clubs. Conservative attire covering shoulders and knees is courteous when visiting old heritage souks or mosques.
  3. Currency & Payments: The UAE Dirham (AED) is pegged to the USD. Credit and debit cards are accepted everywhere, from luxury boutiques to yellow water taxis.
